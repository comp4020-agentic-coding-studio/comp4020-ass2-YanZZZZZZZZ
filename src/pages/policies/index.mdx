# Policies and support

## Late work and extensions

Every workshop artefact (the weekly Death Notes entry, in particular) is due
at the start of the session it feeds — late work loses the chance to be
peer-reviewed that week, which is most of its value. The four weighted
assessments (Boss Blueprint, Loadout Pitch, Death Notes, Final Project)
accept a standard five-day extension for documented illness or hardship, no
questions asked at the point of request; anything longer goes through the
university's standard extension process. Ask before the deadline, not
after — a request the day a piece is due is treated as a late submission,
not an extension.

## Academic integrity

Playing, reading about, or watching footage of a game you haven't personally
finished is expected and encouraged — this course is explicit that evidence
can come from footage, timestamped. What isn't acceptable is presenting
someone else's specific argument (a lecture's reading of a boss, a
classmate's item description, a published analysis) as your own without
naming it. Citing a source you disagree with and explaining why is a
stronger submission than silently avoiding the source altogether.

Generative-AI tools may be used for research and drafting support, but the
argument itself — which moment you chose, why it counts as legible or
arbitrary failure, what the design decision actually was — has to be yours.
A submission that reads as generic genre commentary, with no specific
in-game moment a marker can check, is marked down regardless of how it was
produced.

## Getting help

Ask in the session first — most workshop questions are faster to resolve
with the room's shared examples in front of everyone. Outside sessions,
email the teacher who runs that week (listed on [People](/people/)) for
anything specific to a submission, or the convenor for anything about the
course's shape: schedule, marking criteria, or a brief that seems to
contradict itself.

## Administration

**Enrolment and withdrawal** follow the university's standard census dates;
this course sets no earlier internal deadline for either.

**Special consideration** (illness, hardship, or a circumstance affecting
more than one assessment) goes through the university's standard
special-consideration process rather than a course-specific form — the
convenor is notified automatically and will follow up about extensions
directly.

**Feedback on marking** is available by email within two weeks of a result
being released; a request to re-mark an assessment should name the specific
criterion in dispute, not ask for a general second look.
