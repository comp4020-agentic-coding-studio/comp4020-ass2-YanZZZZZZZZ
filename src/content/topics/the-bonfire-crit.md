---
title: The bonfire crit
description:
  Why a closing critique session should be judged like the genre it studies
  — legible failure, not politeness, and not demolition either
tags: [pedagogy, critique]
related:
  - lectures/week-12
---

A crit session run purely on encouragement teaches nothing a rejection
teaches; a crit session run purely on demolition teaches nothing a
compliment couldn't have said faster. This course has spent eleven weeks
arguing that a well-designed failure is legible — the player can name the
exact decision that caused it — and a closing crit should hold itself to
the identical standard. Feedback that says a one-pager "doesn't work" is
the cheating case: a bigger number (harsher tone) standing in for actual
information. Feedback that says which specific part of the map sketch
contradicts the item descriptions is the punishing case: it costs the
presenter something to hear, and it tells them exactly what to fix.

The closing address at a bonfire crit — this course's version of a
send-off — is not a summary of the semester's content. It's closer to
kindling a fire that's about to go out on its own: the twelve weeks of
scaffolding (lectures, workshops, spec checks) end here, and whatever
judgment a student has built up about legible versus arbitrary difficulty
has to now run without them. The point of naming that plainly, rather than
letting the course just end, is the same point [minimal
UI](/topics/minimal-ui/) makes about a HUD: the scaffolding was only ever
there to build a habit of attention that should outlast the scaffolding
itself.
