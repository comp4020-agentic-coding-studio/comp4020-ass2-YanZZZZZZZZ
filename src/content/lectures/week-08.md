A bonfire isn't a checkpoint — it's a checkpoint, an enemy reset, and a
currency wager bolted into one irreversible act. The full argument for why
that fusion, not the respawn itself, is the actual invention is in
[The bonfire economy](/topics/the-bonfire-economy/).

## The argument

Hollow Knight builds its own version of the same tension without borrowing
Dark Souls' bonfire at all: die, and your geo drops as a Shade occupying
the exact spot you died, guarding it and blocking your path back. Recover
it and you're whole; die again before you reach it and the geo is gone for
good, replaced by an even more dangerous Shade — a second, harsher version
of the same "walk back into the danger that killed you, on the clock"
structure this course's reference case builds around bonfires. Different
skeleton, identical bet: comfort recovered has to be walked back into risk
to be banked.

Not every currency-loss mechanic earns the name "economy" by this course's
standard. A system where lost currency respawns automatically after a
timer, or where the amount at stake is capped so low it never threatens a
meaningful purchase, produces a scare with no lasting cost every time —
insurance with no premium. The levelling cost curve is what turns a
currency mechanic from decoration into tuning: if the number required to
level scales sharply enough that a single lost run represents a real
setback, the stake is legible; if it barely matters at any point in the
game, the "economy" is a coat of paint on a system that was never actually
risking anything.

## Outline

- three separate decisions — respawn location, enemy state, currency risk —
  fused into one lit fire
- Hollow Knight's Shade as a second version of the same structure, built on
  a different skeleton
- loss aversion as the mechanism: a scare that resolves versus a loss that
  actually lands
- the failure case: currency that respawns on a timer or is capped too low
  to matter, insurance with no premium
- the levelling cost curve as the tuning knob underneath the whole economy
- this week's workshop: designing a death-currency mechanic and calculating
  its risk leverage

## Into the session

If you've played a game with a death-currency mechanic — Elden Ring's
runes, Hollow Knight's geo, a roguelike's gold-drop-on-death — bring one
moment where the loss actually landed and one where it didn't. In the
session you'll design your own mechanic and calculate its risk leverage in
concrete terms: what percentage of a player's current wealth is on the
line at a typical death, and whether that leverage changes meaningfully
across a playthrough or stays flat and decorative.
