Fifty points, no dead ones: a player's attribute allocation is closer to a
character sheet than a damage calculation, and every point that does
nothing for the persona it's supposed to be building is the system failing
that reading, not the player wasting a choice. The full argument for why,
and for what a "no dead points" stat system actually has to guarantee, is
in [Build as projection](/topics/build-as-persona/).

## The argument

Dark Souls' stat thresholds are what make an allocation legible as a
choice rather than a slider: a weapon's strength or dexterity requirement
is a visible gate, so putting a point into strength isn't a smooth
diminishing-returns nudge, it's the specific moment a colossal weapon
becomes usable at all. Compare a system that fails this test — a stat whose
only function is a flat, linear bonus with no threshold moment anywhere in
the game. Nothing unlocks; the stat just gets slightly less bad to ignore
the more you invest, which is a tax dressed as a choice, not a persona
option.

The alternative build is where the "no dead points" claim gets tested for
real. A pure-faith caster who never swings a melee weapon, or a bleed-and-
arcane build whose damage arrives in unreliable procs rather than steady
hits, both accept a real, nameable cost — lower baseline damage, uselessness
against bleed-immune bosses — for a persona a min-maxed quality build
wouldn't have. The pitch for a build like that can't be "this is secretly
optimal," because it usually isn't; it has to be "this is who you'd be if
you played this way," and naming the cost honestly is what separates that
pitch from marketing copy.

## Outline

- why an allocation reads as identity when the system is legible enough to
  see the trade-off
- stat thresholds as the mechanism: a weapon requirement is a visible gate,
  not a diminishing-returns curve
- the failure case: a flat linear bonus with no threshold moment, a tax
  dressed as a choice
- "no dead points" as a design requirement, not a balance nicety
- the alternative build as the sharpest test case: a bleed or faith build
  that accepts a real cost a min-maxed build wouldn't
- why a pitch that only lists upside hasn't found the actual trade-off yet
- this week's workshop, and the Loadout Pitch it feeds: 50 points, an
  unconventional build, and the pitch copy that sells it

## Into the session

Bring one stat, from any Souls-like's system, with a reputation for being
weak or a trap — and a guess at why players call it that. In the session
you'll allocate a 50-point budget around that stat, draft the persona it
implies, and write a first pass at pitch copy that names both what the
build lets you do and the specific cost it accepts. The room will call out
any pitch that only lists upside.
