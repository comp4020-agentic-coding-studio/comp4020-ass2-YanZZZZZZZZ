A boss fight is a scene, not a harder enemy — health and damage buy room
for an argument, not just a longer grind. The full case for treating a
phase transition as a dramatic beat that has to be earned before it's
triggered is in
[Boss design as dramatic climax](/topics/boss-design-as-climax/).

## The argument

Dark Souls' Ornstein and Smough is the clearest example of a transition
that's inseparable from its narrative justification, because the
justification is the mechanic: kill one, and the other visibly absorbs
their fallen partner's power — Smough grows and slows further into raw
threat, or Ornstein crackles with borrowed lightning and speeds up into
something closer to what Smough used to threaten. There's no separate
cutscene explaining the spike, because the absorption **is** the spike,
staged in a way the fight has already trained you to read as significant.
Sekiro's Isshin fight does the same work across three phases instead of
one transition — a retired swordsman's memory of his younger self
resurfacing, then the mythic "Sword Saint" title made literal in a burst of
lightning — each escalation legible as the same character remembering more
of who he was, not a difficulty slider someone nudged between phases.

The failure case is the enrage timer: health crosses a threshold, damage
and speed increase, nothing about the arena, the moveset's texture, or the
boss's presented character changes to justify it. It's the damage-sponge
problem from Week 1 wearing a phase counter — a number got bigger, and the
game is asking the cutscene (or the absence of one) to do work it never
did. The test is the same one this course keeps returning to: can you say,
in one sentence, why *this* boss specifically would fight differently once
wounded? If the sentence is "it gets angrier," you have an enrage timer,
not a transition.

## Outline

- why "more health, more damage" produces the damage-sponge cheating case,
  not a boss
- Ornstein and Smough: a transition where the mechanic and the narrative
  justification are the same event
- Isshin's three-phase escalation as memory made literal, not a slider
- the enrage-timer failure: a number changes with no staging or moveset
  change to justify it
- the transition as a scene's turn: narrative justification before
  mechanical trigger
- staging the arena change alongside the rule change, so the spike reads as
  diegetic rather than imposed
- this week's workshop, and the Boss Blueprint it feeds directly: design a
  two-phase boss, its transition trigger, and the one sentence that
  justifies it

## Into the session

Bring the boss from Week 2 and, if you built one for the same boss, the
attack-tells cheatsheet from Week 4. In the session you'll design the
transition trigger, the one sentence that justifies it given what your
item descriptions already established, and one new, telegraphed attack for
phase two — then trade with a partner who has to guess, from the trigger
and staging alone, what kind of character your boss is.
