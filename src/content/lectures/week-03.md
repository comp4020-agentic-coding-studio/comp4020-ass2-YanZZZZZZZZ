A shortcut only earns its name in a map that folds back on itself, and a
level only earns the right to skip a minimap if its architecture can guide a
player by itself. Put a signpost over either one and you've admitted the
geography failed. Firelink Shrine's hub-and-shortcut design and the genre's
refusal of a quest arrow are the same design commitment seen from two
angles, and the full argument — light, bonfires, corpses and distant
landmarks doing the guiding a UI layer would otherwise do — is in
[Level design as argument](/topics/level-design-and-geography/).

## The argument

Most genres solve "where do I go" with a screen: a full-map overlay with
markers, the kind Metroid Prime hands you almost immediately, converting
exploration into fog-of-war clearing against a layout you can already see
in miniature. Dark Souls refuses that screen entirely, betting the whole
argument on architecture instead — which is a bigger bet than "no minimap"
sounds like, because it means every route has to teach its own shape
through what a player can see and feel walking it, with no fallback if the
teaching fails.

Hollow Knight is the useful middle case, because it doesn't refuse a map
outright — it makes the map diegetic instead. You buy blank paper from
Cornifer and fill it in by hand, one room at a time, and even once it
exists it won't show your live position without a specific charm equipped.
The map returns, but only after being demoted from "answer" to "artefact
you have to earn and still interpret," which is close to what this week's
soft-guidance argument asks of light, bonfire glow and corpse loot: not the
absence of information, but information that still requires the player to
look.

## Into the session

Pick one area from any Souls-like you remember well enough to sketch from
memory — don't look anything up. In the session you'll hand-draw a micro
dungeon with at least two shortcut loops, each one a connection a player
would have to earn by walking the long way first, then redesign a cluttered
map screenshot using only lighting, colour and composition — no new icon,
arrow or label allowed.
