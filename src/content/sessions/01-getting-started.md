## Before the session

Play (or watch, timestamped) one attempt-and-death cycle against two cases:
something widely agreed to be punishing-but-fair (Sekiro's Lady Butterfly is
the default; substitute your own if you have a better one), and something
you'd call cheap — a fight or obstacle where the failure felt arbitrary
rather than earned.

## In the session

We build a shared log on the board: for each case, the moment of death, and
the very next thing the game showed the player. Fair cases tend to produce
a specific, nameable lesson ("the second hit lands half a second after the
first — I watched for one and ate two"). Cheap cases tend to produce a
shrug. That gap is the actual data this week collects.

## Afterwards

Keep your log. Every later week's workshop asks you to design something
that produces the fair-case shrug-free reaction rather than the cheap-case
shrug, and this is the first time you'll have named, in your own words,
what the difference actually feels like from the player's seat.
