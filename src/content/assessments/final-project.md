## The brief

> Design a complete opening level for an original Souls-like: the first ten
> minutes a player would experience, argued as a single design document.

This is the semester's four subsystems — level geography, combat, boss
design, and weak guidance — applied to one original level rather than
four separate exercises. Reuse what you've already built: the boss from
[Week 2](/sessions/02-risk-ledger/) and its [Blueprint](/assessments/boss-blueprint/),
the shortcut sketch from [Week 3](/sessions/03-map-and-guidance/), and the
persona from your [Loadout Pitch](/assessments/loadout-pitch/) if it fits
the level you're designing.

## What you submit

A one-pager with four required parts:

1. a hand-drawn or software map sketch of the opening level, with at least
   two shortcut loops
2. two item descriptions from that level, written as unreliable testimony
   per [Week 2](/topics/death-as-narrative-verb/)'s standard
3. a summary of one boss's moves, including its phase transition and the
   sentence that justifies it narratively
4. a short script for the level's opening minute, showing how a player is
   guided toward the first room using only diegetic cues — light, a corpse,
   a distant landmark — with no on-screen objective text

Alongside the one-pager, a 200-word reflection: the one part you rewrote
after noticing it didn't actually match the other three, and what the
mismatch was. Assembling four subsystems into one document is exactly
where a level stops arguing one thing and starts arguing four — the
reflection is where you show you caught that before a reader had to.

## Where this goes next

Nowhere — this is the semester's argument, assembled. It's presented and
peer-reviewed at the Week 12 bonfire crit alongside your
[Death Notes](/assessments/death-notes/) portfolio.
