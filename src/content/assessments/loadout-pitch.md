## The brief

> Given 50 attribute points, design an unconventional build and write the
> pitch copy that would sell it to a skeptical player.

Week 7's lecture argues that a stat allocation is a persona a player
projects onto the game, not just a spreadsheet optimisation. Your job is to
design a build that commits to an identity a min-maxed build wouldn't —
Sekiro's pure-prosthetic-tool run is the reference case, but pick your own.
The build has to accept a real cost for that identity: naming the cost is
part of the persuasion, not a weakness to hide.

## What you submit

A 50-point attribute allocation (name the game or state your own system),
150–250 words of pitch copy written to sell a skeptical player on trying
it, and one sentence naming the specific weakness the build accepts in
exchange for its identity.

Alongside the pitch, a 120-word reflection: the moment during design where
the persona and the statistically stronger allocation pulled in different
directions, and which one you let win. The pitch shows the build you
landed on; the reflection is the only place that shows you noticed the
trade-off rather than stumbling into it.

## Where this goes next

A build with a clear persona is a strong seed for the opening level in the
[Final Project](/assessments/final-project/) — several students design
their one-pager around a level that specifically rewards the build they
pitched here.
